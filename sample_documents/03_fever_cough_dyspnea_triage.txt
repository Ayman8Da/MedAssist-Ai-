TITLE: Triage of Fever, Cough, and Shortness of Breath in Adults

1. PURPOSE

This document describes the clinical information a professional should collect
when an adult presents with fever, cough, and shortness of breath, and the
warning signs that require escalation. It is a triage aid, not a diagnostic
algorithm.

2. IMMEDIATE PHYSIOLOGICAL MEASUREMENTS

The following must be obtained before any diagnostic reasoning is attempted:
- Temperature, measured, not estimated.
- Heart rate and rhythm.
- Respiratory rate, counted over a full minute.
- Blood pressure, with manual confirmation if abnormal.
- Oxygen saturation on room air.
- Peak expiratory flow if the patient can perform it safely.
- Level of consciousness using a validated scale.

A respiratory rate above 24 breaths per minute, oxygen saturation below 92
percent, systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg, or new confusion are escalation
triggers.

3. CLINICAL INFORMATION REQUIRED FOR ASSESSMENT

- Symptom onset and tempo: sudden versus progressive.
- Sputum production, colour, and volume.
- Haemoptysis.
- Pleuritic chest pain.
- Ability to lie flat, and orthopnoea.
- Recent travel, sick contacts, and occupational exposure.
- Immunisation status.
- Comorbidities: asthma, COPD, heart failure, diabetes, chronic kidney disease,
  immunosuppression.
- Smoking history and occupational exposures.
- Pregnancy status where applicable.
- Current medications, particularly anticoagulants and immunosuppressants.

4. DIFFERENTIAL CONSIDERATIONS TO KEEP OPEN

Pneumonia, viral respiratory infection including influenza and SARS-CoV-2,
acute bronchitis, asthma exacerbation, COPD exacerbation, pulmonary embolism,
acute heart failure, and sepsis from a non-respiratory source. A single
narrative rarely distinguishes these reliably. Clinicians should avoid early
closure on one diagnosis.

5. WARNING SIGNS

Respiratory:
- Increasing work of breathing, accessory muscle use, tripod positioning.
- Inability to complete a sentence.
- Silent chest on auscultation.
- Cyanosis.

Circulatory:
- Hypotension or a rising tachycardia.
- Capillary refill longer than three seconds.
- Cool peripheries with mottling.

Neurological:
- New confusion, agitation, or drowsiness.

Other:
- Oliguria.
- Lactate above 2 mmol/L where measured.
- Rapid deterioration over hours.

6. GUIDELINES TO CONSULT

Clinicians should consult their local respiratory infection pathway, local
antimicrobial stewardship guidance, and sepsis screening policy. National
sources commonly referenced include WHO respiratory infection guidance, CDC
seasonal influenza and COVID-19 pages, and NICE guidance on pneumonia and
sepsis. Local resistance patterns often determine empirical antibiotic choice
and should take precedence over generic recommendations.

7. MISSING INFORMATION IN TYPICAL SHORT VIGNETTES

- Objective vital signs.
- Duration and trajectory of symptoms.
- Comorbidities and immune status.
- Whether the patient has been treated already.
- Whether the patient can be safely observed at home.
- Baseline functional and cognitive status.

8. SAFETY NOTE

Shortness of breath with fever can represent a time-critical emergency. A
decision-support tool must not delay physical assessment or escalation. When
in doubt, escalate.
