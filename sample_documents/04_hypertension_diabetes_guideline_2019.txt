TITLE: Blood Pressure Targets in Adults with Type 2 Diabetes (Fictitious Guideline, 2019 Edition)
SOURCE LABEL: MedAssist Synthetic Library
VERSION: 2019.1
PUBLICATION DATE: 2019-06-30
CATEGORY: cardiology

1. SCOPE

This fictitious guideline addresses blood pressure targets and first-line
therapy for adults aged 18 to 75 years with type 2 diabetes and hypertension.

2. RECOMMENDED TARGET

For all adults with type 2 diabetes and hypertension, the recommended treatment
target is a clinic systolic blood pressure below 130 mmHg and a diastolic blood
pressure below 80 mmHg. The same target applies irrespective of age within the
18 to 75 year range covered here.

3. FIRST-LINE THERAPY

An ACE inhibitor or an angiotensin receptor blocker is recommended as
first-line therapy for all patients in scope, based on a presumed renal and
cardiovascular advantage. Calcium channel blockers are described as a second
line option when ACE inhibitors or ARBs are not tolerated.

4. MONITORING

Home blood pressure monitoring is encouraged. Renal function and potassium
should be checked within two weeks of starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor
or ARB, and then annually.

5. EVIDENCE BASE AS STATED IN THIS FICTITIOUS DOCUMENT

The 2019 edition states that the evidence base consisted mainly of trials
enrolling middle-aged adults with relatively uncomplicated hypertension.
Older adults above 75 years, adults with frailty, and adults with advanced
chronic kidney disease were underrepresented. The document explicitly notes
this limitation and recommends extrapolation with caution.

6. TARGET POPULATION AND LIMITATIONS

- Adults aged 18 to 75 years.
- Type 2 diabetes with confirmed hypertension.
- Excludes pregnancy, advanced chronic kidney disease with eGFR below 30, and
  adults living in residential care.
- Single blood pressure target for the whole population.
- No specific recommendation for frailty or orthostatic hypotension.

7. KNOWN GAPS

The document does not address deprescribing in frail older adults, does not
discuss the risk of falls, and does not stratify by baseline cardiovascular
risk. It also assumes reliable clinic blood pressure measurement.
