TITLE: Metformin in Patients with Impaired Renal Function
SOURCE LABEL: MedAssist Synthetic Library
VERSION: 1.0
PUBLICATION DATE: 2026-02-03
CATEGORY: pharmacology

1. PHARMACOKINETIC BASIS

Metformin is eliminated unchanged by the kidneys through tubular secretion and
glomerular filtration. There is no hepatic metabolism of clinical relevance.
When renal function declines, metformin accumulates. Accumulation is
associated with lactic acidosis, a rare but life-threatening adverse effect.

Because clearance depends on renal function, eGFR is the central laboratory
value in any risk assessment. Serum creatinine alone is insufficient in older
adults with low muscle mass.

2. CONTRAINDICATIONS

Metformin is contraindicated in the following situations:
- eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Severe hypoxia or severe respiratory insufficiency.
- Acute illness with dehydration, shock, or sepsis.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Known hypersensitivity to metformin.

3. WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS AT REDUCED eGFR

- eGFR 30 to 44 mL/min/1.73 m2: initiation is not recommended. Continue only
  if already established, at a reduced dose, with close monitoring of renal
  function, typically every three months.
- eGFR 45 to 59 mL/min/1.73 m2: reassess the dose. Monitor renal function at
  least annually, and more often if other risks are present.
- A rapid fall in eGFR, for example a decrease of more than 30 percent, should
  prompt review regardless of the absolute value.

4. TEMPORARY INTERRUPTION

Metformin should be withheld temporarily during:
- Acute illness with vomiting, diarrhoea, or poor oral intake.
- Dehydration or hypoperfusion.
- Sepsis, hypoxia, or shock.
- Iodinated contrast administration, in accordance with the local contrast
  protocol, typically withholding at the time of the study and restarting
  after renal function is confirmed stable.

5. LACTIC ACIDOSIS WARNING SIGNS

Symptoms are non-specific and easily attributed to other causes:
- Malaise, myalgia, and general weakness.
- Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea.
- Hyperventilation or respiratory distress.
- Hypothermia and hypotension.
- Altered mental status or drowsiness.

Lactic acidosis is a medical emergency requiring immediate discontinuation of
metformin, urgent biochemistry, and in-hospital management.

6. DRUGS AND CONDITIONS THAT INCREASE RISK

- Concurrent nephrotoxic agents such as NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, and
  amphotericin B.
- Diuretics causing volume depletion.
- ACE inhibitors and ARBs during acute illness, when they may worsen renal
  perfusion.
- Radiocontrast media.
- Alcohol misuse, which impairs lactate clearance.
- Surgery and general anaesthesia.

7. MONITORING RECOMMENDATIONS

- Check eGFR before starting metformin and at least annually thereafter.
- Check more frequently, typically every three to six months, when eGFR is
  between 30 and 59.
- Recheck after any acute illness requiring hospital care.
- Document the review decision in the clinical record.

8. INFORMATION TYPICALLY MISSING IN A SHORT CLINICAL VIGNETTE

- The actual eGFR value and its trend.
- Whether the patient is currently acutely unwell.
- Concurrent nephrotoxic medication.
- Recent contrast exposure.
- Liver function and alcohol history.
- Whether the patient is on insulin and how glucose is being monitored.
